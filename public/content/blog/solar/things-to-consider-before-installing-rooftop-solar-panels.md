# Things to Consider Before Installing Rooftop Solar Panels

Installing solar panels is a significant investment that can provide decades of clean energy and savings. Before committing, here are the essential factors Tucson homeowners should evaluate.

## 1. Roof Condition and Age

Your roof must be in good condition before solar installation.

### Key Questions:
- **How old is your roof?** If it needs replacement within 10 years, do that first
- **What material?** Composition shingles, tile, and metal all work well
- **Any damage?** Leaks, damaged shingles, or structural issues must be addressed

### Why It Matters
Removing and reinstalling solar panels for roof repairs is expensive ($1,500-3,000+). A roof inspection before solar installation prevents future headaches.

## 2. Roof Orientation and Shading

Not all roofs are equally suited for solar.

### Ideal Conditions:
- **South-facing sections**: Maximum sun exposure
- **Southwest or west-facing**: Good for afternoon peak production
- **Minimal shading**: Trees, chimneys, or neighboring buildings reduce output
- **Appropriate pitch**: 15-40 degrees is ideal

### Shading Impact
Even partial shading significantly reduces output. Modern microinverters and optimizers minimize this impact, but heavily shaded roofs may not be good candidates.

## 3. Energy Usage Patterns

Understanding your electricity consumption helps size your system correctly.

### Review Your Bills
- Look at 12 months of electricity bills
- Note seasonal variations (summer AC use)
- Calculate average and peak monthly usage

### Right-Sizing the System
- Oversized systems waste money
- Undersized systems miss savings opportunities
- Consider future changes (electric vehicle, pool, home additions)

## 4. Financial Considerations

### Upfront Costs
- Average residential system: $15,000-30,000 before incentives
- After 30% federal tax credit: $10,500-21,000
- Payment options: Cash, solar loan, lease/PPA

### Return on Investment
- Typical payback: 5-8 years in Arizona
- System lifespan: 25-30 years
- Net savings over lifetime: $30,000-75,000+

### Incentives Available
- **Federal Investment Tax Credit**: 30% of installation cost
- **Arizona incentives**: May include additional credits
- **Utility rebates**: Check with your power company

## 5. Ownership vs. Leasing

### Buying (Cash or Loan)
**Pros**:
- Maximum long-term savings
- You own the equipment
- Increases home value
- All incentives go to you

**Cons**:
- Higher upfront investment
- You're responsible for maintenance

### Leasing or PPA
**Pros**:
- Little to no upfront cost
- Maintenance typically included

**Cons**:
- Less lifetime savings
- Leasing company gets incentives
- May complicate home sale
- Locked into long-term contract

## 6. Net Metering Policies

Net metering determines how you're credited for excess solar electricity.

### How It Works
- Excess solar power flows to the grid
- Utility credits your account
- Credits offset electricity you use at night/cloudy days

### Arizona Considerations
- Policies vary by utility
- Credit rates may differ from retail rates
- Time-of-use plans affect value
- Demand charges may apply for some customers

## 7. Equipment Quality

Not all solar equipment is equal.

### Panels
- **Tier 1 manufacturers**: LG, Panasonic, SunPower, REC, etc.
- **Efficiency ratings**: 19-22% is standard for quality panels
- **Warranties**: 25 years is standard

### Inverters
- **String inverters**: Cost-effective, good for unshaded roofs
- **Microinverters**: Better for shading, individual panel monitoring
- **Optimizers**: Balance of cost and per-panel optimization

### Mounting Hardware
- Quality racking prevents leaks and damage
- Proper installation is critical

## 8. Installer Selection

Choosing the right installer is as important as choosing equipment.

### What to Look For:
- Licensed, bonded, and insured
- NABCEP certification preferred
- Strong local reputation
- Transparent pricing
- Quality workmanship warranty
- Responsive customer service

### Red Flags:
- High-pressure sales tactics
- Prices significantly below market
- Reluctance to provide references
- Unclear warranty terms

## 9. Permits and HOA Approval

### Permits Required
- Building permit
- Electrical permit
- Utility interconnection agreement

Your installer should handle all permitting.

### HOA Considerations
- Arizona law protects solar rights, but HOAs can set reasonable guidelines
- Submit plans for approval before installation
- Some restrictions on placement may apply

## 10. Maintenance Requirements

Solar panels require minimal but regular maintenance.

### Typical Needs:
- Annual visual inspection
- Occasional cleaning (rain usually suffices in Tucson)
- Monitoring system performance
- Inverter replacement (once in system lifetime)

### Warranties Cover:
- Panel defects (25 years typical)
- Inverter (12-25 years depending on type)
- Workmanship (varies by installer)

## Making Your Decision

Solar is right for most Tucson homeowners who:
- Own their home
- Have a suitable roof
- Pay significant electricity bills
- Plan to stay for 5+ years
